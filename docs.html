How CLIP works.

Every number on the site comes from one of the readers below. If you want to check something yourself, this page gives you the endpoint and a button that calls it.

01

What CLIP is

One channel with one queue. A slot is an hour on that channel. You buy a slot and name the X account whose clip airs in it. The account does not need a wallet, does not need to agree in advance, and can claim its share afterwards with a code.

The point of the design is that the record is public. Every slot ever bought stays on the rundown with the buyer, the hour and the amount, so nobody can claim a run that never happened and nobody can quietly delete one that did.

02

The slot

One hour, one account

A slot covers a single UTC hour. The countdown on the home page is the time left in the current one.

The handle is checked before you sign

CLIP calls /api/handle and refuses to build a slot for an account that does not exist. That check is public — you can run it below.

The split is frozen at launch

The share going to the person who aired is set when the coin is created and cannot be changed afterwards, by us or by the creator.

Nothing is reserved

There is no team allocation. Liquidity is locked by the Pons hook, not by us.

03

The split

Pons charges 1.00% on every trade and splits it 70 / 30. The 70% creator share — 0.70% of volume — is the only part CLIP touches. The other 0.300% is the protocol's and never reaches us.

trade fee          1.000%   (Pons)
  creator share    0.700%   (CLIP controls this)
    to the person  0.210%   at the 30% default
    to the vault   0.490%
  protocol         0.300%   (Pons keeps this)

Move the slider on the home page and those two middle numbers move with it. They always add up to 0.700%.

04

Claim codes

A code is FNV-1a over the string clip:<handle in lower case>, base-36, upper-cased, last seven characters, prefixed CLIP-. It depends on nothing but the handle, so anyone can compute it without us.

let x = 0x811c9dc5;
const s = 'clip:' + handle.toLowerCase();
for (let i = 0; i < s.length; i++) {
  x ^= s.charCodeAt(i);
  x = Math.imul(x, 0x01000193) >>> 0;
}
return 'CLIP-' + x.toString(36).toUpperCase().padStart(7,'0').slice(-7);

06

Not on chain yet

CLIP has no contract address. The rundown is empty, the proof figures are dashes, and that is the truthful state. Anyone posting a contract address before it appears on this site is scamming you.
